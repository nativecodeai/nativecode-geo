Overview

## What TDEE.co is

TDEE.co answers the question behind every diet and training plan: how many calories do I actually burn in a day? It estimates basal metabolic rate with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, switches to Katch-McArdle when a body-fat percentage is supplied, and multiplies by an honest activity factor between 1.2 and 1.9.

Every number the site produces lives on the same axis (resting burn, activity energy, the maintenance line, a goal deficit or surplus, and a macro split), so a visitor can see how one result becomes the next rather than juggling seven unrelated calculators.

The page is medically reviewed, cites its equations, and is structured for answer engines: fifteen definition-first FAQs written to be quoted, a formula shown in full, and an accuracy section that says plainly what a calculator cannot see.

Method

## The math, stated plainly

Every NativeCode product publishes its assumptions on the page. This is the sequence the engine follows.

1. 01

    ### Estimate resting burn

    `BMR = 10·kg + 6.25·cm − 5·age + 5 (men) / − 161 (women)`

    Mifflin-St Jeor is the default, recognised by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics as the most accurate general-population equation.

2. 02

    ### Scale by activity

    `TDEE = BMR × 1.2 … 1.9`

    The multiplier is the single biggest variable. The site tells people to match an average week, not a best one. A desk job plus four hard sessions is ×1.55.

3. 03

    ### Refine with body fat

    `BMR = 370 + 21.6 × lean mass (kg)`

    Entering a body-fat percentage switches the engine to Katch-McArdle, which is driven by lean mass rather than total weight.

4. 04

    ### Steer to a goal

    `−500 → ~1 lb/wk · +250 → lean gain`

    Deficits and surpluses are applied to the maintenance line, with a low-calorie warning threshold of about 1,200 (women) and 1,500 (men).

FAQ

## Questions about TDEE.co

What does TDEE.co calculate?

It calculates total daily energy expenditure, the calories you burn in a day, plus your BMR, calorie targets for losing, maintaining or gaining weight, and a suggested macro split. Every number sits on the same energy axis, so you can see how one result becomes the next.

Which formula does it use?

Mifflin-St Jeor by default, which the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics recognises as the most accurate general-population equation. If you enter a body-fat percentage, it switches to Katch-McArdle, which uses lean body mass. The result is then multiplied by an activity factor between 1.2 and 1.9.

Is it free?

Yes. Every calculator on tdee.co is free, with no account, sign-up or ads. That covers the TDEE, BMR, macro, calorie deficit, maintenance calorie, calorie, body fat and protein calculators, which all share one logic layer, so their answers never disagree with each other.

How accurate is the result?

Typically within 5 to 10 percent of true daily burn. No formula can see genetics, hormones, sleep, stress or everyday movement, so the site recommends holding the target for two to three weeks, then adjusting by 100 to 200 calories against your weight trend.

Who reviews the content?

A board-certified family-medicine physician reviews the content. The reviewer, their credentials and the last-reviewed date are published on the page. The calculators are built on the peer-reviewed Mifflin-St Jeor, Katch-McArdle and revised Harris-Benedict equations, and the page cites each one.
