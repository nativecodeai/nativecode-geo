# PeriodCalculator.ai Ovulation Calculator

Overview

## What PeriodCalculator.ai is

PeriodCalculator.ai takes three inputs (first day of the last period, average cycle length, and period length) and returns a calibrated forecast: next period, ovulation, fertile window, and the current cycle phase, drawn on a dial rather than dumped in a table.

The math is stated plainly on the page. Ovulation is estimated as the projected next period minus the luteal phase, because the luteal phase is far steadier than the cycle itself. The luteal length is adjustable for people who have confirmed theirs with ovulation tests.

The site has three modes (tracking, trying to conceive, avoiding pregnancy) and is explicit that it is an awareness tool, not contraception. It cites large-scale cycle datasets, names its clinical thresholds, and is written to clinical-review standards because reproductive health is YMYL territory.

Method

## The math, stated plainly

Every NativeCode product publishes its assumptions on the page. This is the sequence the engine follows.

1. 01

    ### Project the next period

    `next period = last period + cycle length`

    A cycle is counted from the first day of bleeding to the day before the next bleed. The site projects three cycles, not one.

2. 02

    ### Count back to ovulation

    `ovulation = next period − luteal phase`

    The luteal phase holds near 14 days for most people while the follicular phase absorbs the variation, so counting backward is more reliable than the day-14 rule.

3. 03

    ### Bracket the fertile window

    `window = 5 days before ovulation + ovulation day`

    Sperm survive up to five days; the egg about one. Conception is likeliest in the final two days.

4. 04

    ### Calibrate to real data

    In an analysis of more than 600,000 cycles, only a minority were exactly 28 days. Entering real averages instead of defaults is what narrows the window.

FAQ

## Questions about PeriodCalculator.ai

How does it estimate ovulation?

By subtracting the luteal phase, 14 days by default and adjustable, from the projected next period. The luteal phase is far steadier than the cycle itself, so counting back is more reliable than the day-14 rule. On a 35-day cycle, estimated ovulation lands around day 21, not day 14.

Can it be used to avoid pregnancy?

No. The site is explicit that a calendar calculator is an awareness tool, not contraception, and its avoid-pregnancy mode shows estimated risk only. Ranges are shown as ranges, estimates are labelled as estimates, and the page lists clinical thresholds that are reasons to see a clinician.

Is anything I enter saved?

No. Every calculation runs in the browser, and nothing is transmitted, stored or tracked. PeriodCalculator.ai is private by architecture, so it shows the next three periods and the current cycle phase without an account or a tracking pixel. Your dates stay on your device.

How accurate is it?

With regular cycles, predictions are typically within a day or two. With irregular cycles, the site tells you to read each date as the center of a window. Entering your real average cycle and period length instead of the defaults is what narrows the window, since few cycles are exactly 28 days.

Is it medically reviewed?

Review by a board-certified OB-GYN is in progress, and the page says so. The reviewer byline publishes at launch. In the meantime the methodology cites ACOG patient guidance and large-scale cycle datasets published in peer-reviewed journals, and the content is written to clinical-review standards.
