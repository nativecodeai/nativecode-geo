Method

## The math, stated plainly

Every NativeCode product publishes its assumptions on the page. This is the sequence the engine follows.

1. 01

    ### Measure the band

    `band = underbust, snug, rounded to nearest even`

    The snug ribcage measurement is the band. The modern method does not add four inches. That rule dates from stiffer fabrics and is the most common source of wrong sizes.

2. 02

    ### Measure the bust

    `cup = bust − band · 1″ per cup`

    A loose measurement across the fullest point. One inch of difference is an A, two is a B, three a C, four a D, then DD and beyond.

3. 03

    ### Convert across regions

    `US 34C → EU 75C → FR 90C → AU 12C`

    Band numbers change by system (inches, centimetres, or dress-size-style numbers), and cup ladders diverge above D. The converter handles both.

4. 04

    ### Offer sister sizes

    `32D ≈ 34C ≈ 36B`

    Cup volume is relative to band. Going down a band and up a cup keeps the volume the same, which is how a shopper fixes a brand that runs tight.

FAQ

## Questions about Brasize.ai

What do I need to use the calculator?

Two measurements: a snug underbust, taken level around the ribcage directly under the bust, and a loose bust measurement across the fullest point with the tape resting, not pulled. Inches or centimetres both work. The calculator turns the underbust into the band and the bust-band difference into the cup.

Which sizing systems does it return?

Brasize.ai returns US, UK, EU, AU and FR sizes, each with band, cup, full size and sister sizes. Showing the same size in every system a shopper will meet means an online order from a foreign brand lands right. The converter also handles cup ladders that diverge above D.

What is a sister size?

A sister size has the same cup volume on a different band. A 34C, 32D and 36B hold roughly the same volume, so going down a band and up a cup keeps the volume the same. Shoppers use sister sizes when a band runs tight or loose in a particular brand.

Why doesn't it add four inches to the band?

Because that rule dates from an era of non-stretch fabrics and produces bands that are too loose. Brasize.ai uses the modern snug-measure method, where the snug ribcage measurement is the band. The site explains the old rule and retires it, since it is the most common source of wrong sizes.

Is it free?

Yes. Brasize.ai is free to use with no sign-up. Visitors can run the calculator and browse the sizing reference, which runs to more than 700 pages covering sizes by region, cup-size charts, breast shapes, bra styles and brand-by-brand comparisons, without creating an account.
