## How do you run a fair agency search?

The template does the heavy lifting. These are the habits that make the difference between a confident decision and a beauty contest.

01

### Write a short brief

Goals with numbers, scope, budget range and timeline. Five clear pages beat a thirty-page questionnaire.

02

### Send it to three to five agencies

Enough to compare, few enough that each agency puts real effort into its answer.

03

### Score separately, then compare

Each reviewer fills in the scorecard alone before the group meets, so the loudest voice does not set the scores.

04

### Call the references

Ask who did the work, how problems were handled and whether they would hire the agency again.

## Digital marketing RFP questions

### What should a digital marketing RFP include?

A digital marketing RFP should include a short company overview, the problem you want solved, goals with current and target numbers, the services in scope, your current tools and team, a budget range, a timeline, what each proposal must contain and how you will score it.

### How many agencies should receive the RFP?

Three to five agencies is enough for most companies. More than that adds review work without improving the decision, and agencies put less effort into crowded pitches. Send the same brief to each one, give them the same deadline and the same chance to ask questions, then score every proposal on the same scorecard.

### Should we share our budget in the RFP?

Yes, share it as a range. Without one, agencies guess at scope and you get proposals that cannot be compared side by side. A range does not commit you to spending the top of it, and it lets each agency show what it would prioritize at that level of investment.

### How long should the RFP process take?

Plan on four to eight weeks from sending the RFP to signing a contract. Allow two to three weeks for agencies to write proposals, one to two weeks for presentations and reference calls, and time for legal review and contracts. Rushing the proposal window usually leads to less thoughtful responses.
