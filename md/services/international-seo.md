Where it goes wrong

## Why does the wrong page show up in the wrong country?

The wrong page shows up because search engines cannot tell which version is for whom. Most international problems come from structure, not content, so markets end up competing with each other.

01

### Missing or broken hreflang

Without correct hreflang, UK shoppers see US prices and Spanish speakers land on English pages.

02

### Translated, not localized

Direct translation misses the words people actually search in each market, so pages exist but do not rank.

03

### One domain, no local trust

A new market starts with few local links or mentions, so even good pages struggle to rank.

FAQ

## International SEO questions

### Should we use subfolders or country domains?

Subfolders suit most brands. Folders such as /uk/ or /es/ keep all authority on one domain and are easier to manage. Country domains can build more local trust but need their own links and upkeep. We recommend a structure based on your markets, your team and how much each market will be maintained.

### What is hreflang?

Hreflang is a tag that tells search engines which language and country each version of a page is for, so they show the right one to each searcher. It has to be set on every version and point both ways to work. Missing or one-way tags are one of the most common international SEO errors.

### Is translating our site enough?

No. People in each market use their own terms, and some products need different names, prices or proof. Localized pages built on local keyword research rank far better than direct translations. We map demand in each language first, then plan the pages each market actually needs.

### Do you translate the content?

No, we plan it and review it. We set the content, keywords and structure for each market and work with your translators or native-speaking partners on the final language. Then we review every page for search before launch, including titles, headings, hreflang and internal links.
