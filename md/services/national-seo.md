National versus local

## How is national SEO different from local SEO?

National SEO is won on authority, not distance. Local rankings lean on your address and your map listing. National rankings depend on how well your site covers a topic and how much the rest of the web trusts it.

01

### No map to lean on

There is no map pack for national searches. Every result is earned by the page itself and the site behind it.

02

### Bigger competitors

You compete with national brands, marketplaces and review sites that have years of links and content.

03

### More pages to get right

National sites have more categories, products and topics, so structure and crawl control matter far more.

FAQ

## National SEO questions

### What is the difference between local and national SEO?

Local SEO targets searches near a location, and national SEO targets searches across the whole country. Local SEO works mostly through Google Maps, location pages and reviews. National SEO depends on site structure, content depth and links rather than distance, because there is no map pack to lean on.

### How long does national SEO take?

National SEO usually takes six to twelve months to compound in competitive markets. Technical fixes can show results within weeks, because they help pages that already exist. Content and authority take longer to build. We report monthly by page group so you can see which parts of the site are moving.

### Do you work with companies outside Atlanta?

Yes. NativeCode is based in Atlanta and works with clients across the US, mostly remotely. National programs run on shared dashboards, monthly reporting and regular calls with your marketing, product and engineering teams, so location is rarely a factor in how the work gets done.

### Can you run national and local SEO together?

Yes. Multi-location brands often need both: national pages for category and product searches, and location pages and profiles for each branch. We plan them as one program so the national pages and the local pages support each other instead of competing for the same searches.
